## Purpose
Publish the top-level statement of intent that anchors objectives and the supporting topic-specific policies.

## What the standard requires
The policy shall be appropriate to the organization's purpose, include information security objectives or a framework for setting them, include a commitment to satisfy applicable requirements, include a commitment to continual improvement, be documented, communicated within the organization and available to interested parties as appropriate.

## Inputs (from earlier stages)
Stage 1-3 outputs, leadership mandate, legal register.

## Activities
1. Draft a one-to-two page policy in plain language.
2. Reference scope, objectives framework and legal commitments.
3. Get executive review and CEO signature.
4. Publish to all staff and make an external version available to customers.
5. Plan the topic-specific policy set (access control, acceptable use, backup, supplier, etc.) for control implementation.
6. Set annual review and acknowledgement tracking.

## Deliverable
Information Security Policy v1.0 and a topic-policy plan.

## Worked example: Orbitwise Cloud Ltd (fictional)
> **Orbitwise Cloud Ltd Information Security Policy (excerpt)**
> Orbitwise protects the confidentiality, integrity and availability of customer and company information. We commit to satisfy applicable legal, regulatory and contractual requirements, including PDPPL, UK GDPR and customer agreements, and to continually improve our ISMS.
> Objectives are set annually by the Steering Committee and measured through the ISMS scorecard.
> All employees and contractors shall comply with this policy and supporting standards. Breaches may lead to disciplinary action. Exceptions follow the documented exception process.
> Approved by: CEO · Version 1.0 · Review: annually

**Topic-specific policy plan:** access control, acceptable use, information classification, backup, cryptography, supplier security, remote working, incident management, secure development, clear desk and screen.

## Exit criteria
- [ ] All four mandatory content elements of 5.2 are present.
- [ ] Approved by top management, not delegated to IT.
- [ ] Staff acknowledgement rate is tracked.
- [ ] Policy wording uses 'shall' for mandatory statements.

## Common audit findings and how to avoid them
- Policy approved by the IT manager rather than top management.
- No commitment to continual improvement.
- Policy not available to customers who require it.
