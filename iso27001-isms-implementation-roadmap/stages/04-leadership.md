## Purpose
Secure and evidence visible top-management commitment: authority, budget and accountability. Without it every later stage stalls at the resource request.

## What the standard requires
Top management shall demonstrate leadership by ensuring policy and objectives align with strategy, integrating ISMS requirements into business processes, providing resources, communicating importance, ensuring the ISMS achieves its outcomes, directing and supporting people, promoting continual improvement, and supporting other management roles.

## Inputs (from earlier stages)
Business case, scope statement, context and interested-party registers.

## Activities
1. Present the business case: tender requirements, risk exposure, cost and timeline.
2. Obtain a signed ISMS mandate and a named executive sponsor.
3. Approve budget and headcount for the implementation and year-one operation.
4. Set up an ISMS Steering Committee with a charter and monthly cadence.
5. Schedule executive communications on why security matters.
6. Record decisions in minutes; these become audit evidence.

## Deliverable
ISMS Mandate, Steering Committee Charter and signed minutes, resource approval.

## Worked example: Orbitwise Cloud Ltd (fictional)
| Commitment (5.1) | Evidence at Orbitwise |
|---|---|
| Resources provided | Budget of a defined amount approved in Steering Committee minutes; two analyst hires approved |
| Policy aligned to strategy | CEO statement ties ISO 27001 to the bank-customer growth plan |
| Integrated into processes | Security gate added to the product release checklist |
| Communicated importance | CEO all-hands recording and intranet post |
| Promote improvement | Quarterly Steering Committee agenda includes the improvement backlog |

**Steering Committee:** CEO (chair), CTO, CISO, Head of People, Head of Legal, Finance Director. Quorum of four, monthly during implementation, then quarterly.

## Exit criteria
- [ ] Minutes show decisions, not just attendance.
- [ ] Budget approval is dated and traceable.
- [ ] Executives can describe the policy and objectives if interviewed.

## Common audit findings and how to avoid them
- Management commitment is a signed policy only, with no resources or meetings.
- Executives cannot answer basic ISMS questions during the audit interview.
